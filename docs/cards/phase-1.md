## Calls made in the build, for Alan to overrule

1. The follow-up clock stops while a client's change waits on Nelson. A chase is wrong when the next move is ours.
2. The mark-up column shows Nelson's rule, read-only (decision 1).
3. The official quote drops "Company and VAT details" and says "No VAT charged" (brief section 10, row 2).
4. The Portal does not promise a proof "within one working day": decision 6 has no owner yet. It says a proof comes first and nothing is made until it is signed.
5. Product pictures are a colour tile until the customiser publishes mockups. No drawn stand-ins, no generated pictures.
6. Deposit (50% over R 20 000), validity (14 days) and lead time (7 working days) sit in `src/quotes/settings.ts` at the brief's recommended defaults.
7. The design's "Tumbler removed" strip disagreed with its own lines. The stand-in quote keeps the tumbler and its numbers are Nelson's price fixture, so every total adds up.
8. On mock data every signed-in Portal session sees the one sample client (Mother Moon Herbals). The quotes are invented; no client's real data exists yet.
9. The Portal is one calm column (Alan, 21 Nov 2026: it must not read like a spreadsheet). Plain rows, the total and one next step up top, few words. Price breakdown, versions and declining open when asked for. Quantity breaks, unbuilt buttons and unbuilt menu items are off the client's screen until they work.
10. Bare essentials on both surfaces (Alan, 21 Nov 2026: question it, question it again, remove it). Today and the pipeline are headers and rows. The builder leads with the picture, the sell price and the margin; cost, supplier and stock open on request. Explainer text, unbuilt buttons and unbuilt menu items are gone. A thing comes back only when the quoting or delivery flow needs it.
11. A real case sits in the sample book as Q-00014: the Mother Moon Collection as sent in November 2026, twelve pieces with their own mockups and sent prices (Alan, 21 Nov 2026). The document itself is numbered Q-00007, which the contract's fixture quote already holds. Quantities start at 25 each as a placeholder. No cost is invented for these products: the Desk shows the sent price and leaves cost and margin blank until Nelson answers. The mockups are held as data in `src/mock/mother-moon-pictures.ts` and drawn inside pages behind the login, never as public files; they leave the repo when the customiser publishes to private storage.
12. A client tries quantities and drops pieces on the quote itself. The pick rides in the page address and saves nothing. It only lands on a quantity Nelson already quoted, at Nelson's quoted price for it; the total is those quoted prices added up, with set-up and delivery as sent. "Ask Nelson for this" sends it as a change and Nelson answers with a new version. A pick is never paid for directly. Free-typed quantities wait for the live price call.
13. Orders and proofs are on both surfaces on sample data, ahead of the database, so the whole road from quote to delivery can be walked (Alan, 21 Nov 2026: build the other sections so we see how it connects). Eight sample orders, one at every step. Every button runs the real order and proof state machines and saves nothing. The evidence record, the print house's upload link, the waybill's three effects and the reminders land with the database and the outbox.
14. The target date is counted here from Nelson's two numbers held on the order (production and transit, in working days), with South African public holidays in `src/orders/dates.ts`. Sample orders use 7 and 2. When Nelson's delivery estimate is live, its answer replaces the sample numbers; the calendar stays.
15. The proof sheet on a sample order is the product's mockup standing in for the print house's sheet. The signature is typed; a drawn one is an open choice. Only an Owner signs (decision 7, recommended default); on sample data the one person is an Owner.
16. The supply side of an order (supplier, their order number) sits in `src/desk/order-supply.ts` and shows on the Desk's order page only. No cost or margin is shown on an order yet: those are Nelson's answer and arrive with the order pack.
17. Clients on the Desk: one row per client, and a page that holds all of theirs (orders, quotes, contact, numbers, conversations). The numbers are counts and sums of what is on file. "Found us", people with roles, artwork, the mockup shop and notes wait for their own data.
18. The client page is two columns (Alan, 21 Nov 2026): orders and quotes on the left; contact, four numbers and the conversation on the right, always open. The conversation takes a reply and "Send quote", which drops a sent quote of that client into WhatsApp. Both are checks on sample data until the WhatsApp channel is wired (phase 4).
19. Inbox on the Desk: one row per conversation, waiting on us first and oldest at the top, with what was read from the message as a chip and one button. "Start a quote" opens a draft from what was read; an unknown number becomes a new client in Draft. Three sample enquiries stand in for the message store. What is read is words, never a price. Reading by the agent and the live channels land with phase 4.
20. Business and Suppliers on the Desk. Business is sales only: the month's paid orders, average order, what is open, what is out on quote, quotes won, sales by month and the paid list. No cost, profit or margin is shown or worked out here; those are Nelson's answer and arrive with the order pack. Suppliers is who makes it: costing requests with their answer time, then one line per supplier that opens to the orders with them. Two sample costing requests stand in for the request log. Supplier names stay under `src/desk`.
21. Brand kit and Artwork on the Portal, on sample data (brief 3.7). Brand kit is colours with the nearest thread, type and rules as read from the client's guide; anything unsure carries "Guess · confirm" and only an Owner confirms. Artwork is the client's files with their check result (vector or pixels, the largest sharp size, colour count) and Nelson's redraws apart; "Ask for a redraw" shows only on a file that needs one. The kit and the files in the sample are invented: no client's real brand is in the repo. No file is drawn or downloadable yet, and there is no upload: both wait for private storage. The reader, the artwork check and the customiser's `GET /api/v1/brand-kit` land in phase 5.
22. Getting a brand in (Alan, 21 Nov 2026: paste a link and it builds the kit, even if not 100% right; simplest and cheapest first). Three doors in order: paste a website, drop files, pick from a cloud drive. The first is built. `src/brand/read-site.ts` reads a page and its style sheets into a name, a summary, colours (colour before greys), fonts and logo addresses, with no model and no paid service; `src/brand/fetch-site.ts` fetches it: https only, a public host name only, every hop checked against private address ranges, five seconds, size caps, nothing of ours sent. Logos found are carried in the page as data, never linked. Everything read is a guess and nothing is saved until the database lands. The summary is the site's own description for now; a written "flavour" needs the agent (phase 4). Thread names in `src/brand/threads.ts` are a stand-in list of colour words. File drop waits for private storage: an upload is copied in, never linked, so a signed proof always points at a file that cannot change. The live fetch could not be run from the build sandbox; its first live run is on the preview.
23. Artwork is a filing system without folders: each file carries one tag (logo, icon, pattern, photo), one logo is the main logo that mockups start with, and tags filter the list once a client has more than one kind.
24. The mockup shop on the Portal (brief 3.7) is worked out, not stored: every piece on the client's own sent quotes and orders, once each, picture first, ordered ones leading. The price shown is a guide and is always one Nelson quoted, at the quantity he quoted it for ("About R 60,00 each at 50"); nothing is worked out here. "Order again" and "Quote me" take a quantity and open a quote for Nelson to price; they never order or price anything. Saved designs from the customiser, the overnight mockup runner, "Customise" and sharing the shop as a store land with phase 5 and the share links.
